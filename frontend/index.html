Traffic World Model DLR-UT / AIM intersection, Braunschweig

Generator: …

From a few seconds of a real traffic situation observed at the AIM research intersection in Braunschweig, a generative trajectory world model produces several plausible, physically consistent continuations of the scene — shown as a direct bird's-eye-view comparison between the real trajectory and the variants generated by the model.

How it works

1

Observe

Real trajectories from the DLR-UT dataset — cars, bicycles, pedestrians at a densely trafficked urban intersection.

2

Generate

A diffusion model samples several alternative continuations of the entire scene at once — not agent by agent in isolation, but jointly consistent.

3

Compare

Play real and generated variants side by side, time-synced, in the BEV player, and toggle each one on or off.

Value for different audiences

For grant applications & reviewers

Technical proof that a generative traffic world model can be trained on real data collected at your own infrastructure and produce plausible scenario variants — a solid foundation for bigger follow-up stages (e.g. vehicle-side perspectives, photorealistic video generation).

For industry partners

A tangible demonstration, understandable within minutes, of the core principle: learn from real infrastructure data and generate controllable synthetic scenario variants for testing and training purposes — without needing the full pixel-video stack to make the point.
